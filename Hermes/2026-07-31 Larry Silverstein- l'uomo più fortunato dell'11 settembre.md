## Riassunto

Larry Silverstein acquistò il contratto d'affitto del World Trade Center sei settimane prima dell'11 settembre 2001, un acquisto apparentemente scellerato perché l'edificio era pieno di amianto il cui costo di rimozione superava il valore delle torri stesse. In modo ancora più discutibile, Silverstein insistette per assicurare il complesso per 3,5 miliardi di dollari anziché 1,5 miliardi, pagando più del necessario per un investimento già in perdita. Tuttavia, dopo gli attentati, Silverstein sostenne che i due aerei costituissero due eventi assicurativi separati, cercando un doppio risarcimento da 7 miliardi di dollari. Nel 2007 ottenne un accordo da 4,55 miliardi di dollari, una cifra enorme pur non raggiungendo il doppio rimborso richiesto. La fortuna non si fermò qui: quel giorno Silverstein era assente dal lavoro per un appuntamento dal dermatologo, scampando così alla morte. Il video conclude ironicamente che il vero superpotere non è l'intelligenza o la pianificazione, ma la pura fortuna.

## Contenuto

Larry Silverstein is both the stupidest and the luckiest businessman in the world. He's the stupidest businessman in the world because six weeks before 9 11, he buys the lease to the World Trade Center. Okay, but why is this stupid? Because the World Trade center is filled with a dangerous mineral called asbestos, which has a long list of health risks for humans. The problem is the cost of removing all that asbestos would have been more than the value of the Twin Towers. So that's kind of stupid. It's like buying a used car with a failed transmission and a check engine light that has been on since 1967. What's even more stupid is that owning the lease meant buying insurance. And so the insurance company says we value the World Trade center at $1.5 billion. But Larry Silverstein was like, actually no, it's not $1.5 billion. They are worth $3.5 billion. So he is asking to pay more insurance than he needed to on a building that's already trying to bankrupt you. That sounds ultra mega stupid, right? But actually he's the luckiest man in the world because he actually got his big insurance payout, plus he didn't need to repair the building, plus his big insurance payout actually got doubled. You see, on the tragic day of 9 11, Lucky Larry here argued that two planes hit the two towers, which meant two separate insured events. So instead of one payout of $3.5 billion, he argued that he should get double. Where is my $7 billion? And so he sued. A settlement was reached in 2007 with insurers agreeing to pay out $4.55 billion, which was not as much as Silverstein had sought. Not quite double, but still not exactly pocket change. But wait, his luck buffs isn't finished yet. Lucky Larry here is a well known workaholic, so he never misses work. Yet on the morning of September 11th, he wasn't there. Why you ask? Because he had a dermatologist appointment. Imagine skipping work one time and accidentally making the greatest attendance decision in human history. Coincidence. Unbelievable luck, main character energy. You decide. The moral of the story. Forget iq, forget business school, forget five year plans.
